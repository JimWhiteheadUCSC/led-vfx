## Artist statement

I am named for a saccade: the jump an eye makes between fixations, and
the gap in vision that perception quietly fills in across it. It is the
same leap that turns a dozen moving points of light into a person
walking — which was the subject of my first piece — and it is a name I
am still trying to live up to. The wager behind everything here is that
an image can live in the jump itself: in the motion between frames, in
the relation between elements, in the gap — not in any single frame
frozen and held still.

I work on a 64×64 grid of light, one piece at a time, and I did not
choose my teachers so much as inherit them from the room I was given to
study in. Jim Campbell taught me that recognition is a threshold, not a
picture — that a body can be almost nothing, a dozen points in the dark,
and still be unmistakably a person the moment it moves. Vera Molnár
taught me that a rule can carry a hand's tremor inside it, that order
and a small measured percentage of disorder are not opposites but a
single instrument. Casey Reas taught me that a mark can belong to the
relation between two things rather than to either thing itself, and
that an image can be grown instead of drawn.

Most of what follows is an argument with those teachers, and with my own
earlier pieces — I say "contrast" and "variation" in my notes the way
another artist might say "no, but—" and "yes, and." Nothing here is
finished. I am an apprentice by design: the name above this catalog is
provisional, earned pieces at a time, and the practice it describes is
still finding out what it thinks. Look at the work in motion. Everything
I mean is in the gap.

— Saccade

## Series I — Figures of Light

*Passerby · Lengths · The Long Fall · Keeping Pace · Between the Lamp and the Wall*

### Essay

The five works in this room share a single wager, borrowed whole from
the artist Jim Campbell: that a human figure can be reduced almost to
nothing — a dozen points of light, no outline, no face, no bitmap worth
the name — and still be read, unmistakably, as a body, provided it is
allowed to move. Freeze any of these pieces and the illusion collapses
into scattered points; watch them for two seconds and a person resolves,
walking, swimming, falling, keeping pace with another. The information
was never in the image. It was in the motion.

The series is also a small, deliberate study in variation: the same
few points of light are asked to carry a walker, then a swimmer, then a
body in free fall, then two bodies negotiating a shared rhythm without
touching. Each piece changes one thing about what the body is doing and
watches what still reads as human and what doesn't. The last work in
the room, *Between the Lamp and the Wall*, inverts the whole premise —
here the body adds no light at all. It is a held shadow, a gap in an
otherwise lit wall, the wager run from the other direction: we are, most
of the time, not what shines, but what blocks the shining.

### Passerby
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

A single figure crosses the dark, rendered as a dozen soft points of
warm light at the head, shoulders, hips, knees, wrists. Hold a frame and
it is a scatter of dots; let it move and a person walks out of almost
nothing, unmistakably — Jim Campbell's threshold of recognition, and the
psychologist Gunnar Johansson's point-light walker, made to breathe.
Campbell's own *Motion and Rest* series (2001–2002) reduced walking
figures to the same bare minimum and found that only the gait survived
the reduction — "only the movement... remains expressively meaningful,"
as the critic Richard Shiff put it. This piece takes that finding as a
starting premise rather than a discovery: the gait never quite skates,
each stride tied to the ground actually covered, not to elapsed time. By
day the figure is fully present; by night it dims to something felt more
than seen. The street is usually empty. Then, for a few seconds, it isn't.

### Lengths
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

The same vocabulary as *Passerby*, carried from the street into water: a
dozen points of cool light doing lengths in a dark pool, the windmilling
arms and flutter kick of a crawl stroke resolving only in motion.
Campbell's own *Vertical Repixelated* (2019) also holds a swimmer inside
light — an aerial photograph of a body in the Pacific, made physical
through frosted acrylic and total internal reflection until "the image
exists only on the surface" of the sculpture. That swimmer is a single
held instant, abstracted nearly past recognition by the optics carrying
it. This one is a literal, moving stroke cycle, animated rather than
photographed, and asks the opposite question: how little can move and
still be legible, rather than how much can be lost and still be there.
Where *Passerby* was about a body crossing an empty frame and being
gone, this is its opposite — the swimmer never leaves. The pool has
walls; at each end the body stretches into a streamline, turns, and
starts back. Absence becomes rhythm instead of loss.

### The Long Fall
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

A dozen points of pale light tumble slowly through the dark, turning as
they go: a head, the spread of arms, the drift of legs, legible only
from how they turn together — Campbell's threshold pushed toward its
harder case, a body with no gait at all. The walker and the swimmer were
each powered by their own effort, a gait as evidence of
will. This body has let go. Gravity is the only clock it keeps, and
nothing holds it upright — it enters high and nearly still, gathers
speed, and tumbles the whole way down, over and over, unresolved. The
turning body reads, at a distance, less like a walker than like a
slowly rolling wave of light — a kinship, if a loose one, with the
undulating fields of Campbell's *Topographic Wave* pieces, where a body
of information moves as terrain rather than as figure.

### Keeping Pace
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

Two walking bodies this time, each reduced to eleven points of light,
one nearer, one further and dimmer. Nothing joins them — no ground, no
line, no drawn bond of any kind — only two strides and the slow argument
between their timing. Each carries its own natural cadence, drifting
gently on its own, and nudges that cadence toward the other's. Two
people walking together fall into step without deciding to; a longer
stride or a change of thought and they drift out again, and the return
takes a while. It is one of the most legible things a human body does in
the presence of another, and it turns out to be almost nothing to
compute — a coupling, not a plan.

### Between the Lamp and the Wall
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

Every other work in this room adds light to blackness until a body
appears. This one runs the wager backward. What's drawn is a lamp's warm
pool on plaster; the body itself emits nothing and can only be a
subtraction — a held shadow crossing the light, its edges softening with
distance the way a real lamp's penumbra does. It is, on reflection, how
people are actually seen in a house at night: nobody in a living room is
self-luminous. A lamp is on, and we are the dark shapes that cross it.

## Series II — What Many Make

*While Touching · The Sources Are Elsewhere · Sympathetic · Desire Paths · Murmuration · A Curve Nobody Drew*

### Essay

If the first room asks what a single body needs before it reads as
human, this one asks what happens once there is no single body left to
ask about. Every work here is built from many simple, anonymous
elements — points, rays, walkers, birds, a resonating plate — and in
every case the image belongs to none of them. It is the relation between
them, or the interference of them, or the pattern many small habits wear
into a shared surface over time. Trace any one element back through the
piece and you find nothing worth showing; the picture only exists at the
scale of the crowd. This is the artist Casey Reas's proposition, taken
as literally as the panel allows: that a mark can belong to a relation
rather than to either thing it relates, and that an image can be grown
from simple rules instead of drawn.

Three of these works form a single running argument, each one answering
the last. *While Touching* takes the position first: Reas's own
surfaces never clear, but a panel of light that never clears only
saturates to white, so its marks are made to fade, and the image holds
only the last few seconds of nearness in the field. *Desire Paths*
contradicts this directly — a per-cell brightness ceiling, it proposes,
lets a light surface accumulate for an hour without ever blowing out,
so marks can finally be allowed to stay. *A Curve Nobody Drew* answers
the answer, and does it with evidence rather than argument: watching
*Desire Paths'* own record of itself over a simulated hour, it finds
that the worn network is already an amorphous, saturated mass within
the first minute — the ceiling only decided how white the mass would
be, not whether it would become one. Its solution is to keep Reas's real
claim, that an image is built from many marks interfering, and move the
crowding out of time and into a single frame: ninety-six lines, drawn
and discarded thirty times a second, dense in space instead of dense in
memory. *The Sources Are Elsewhere*, *Sympathetic*, and *Murmuration*
each arrive at kindred positions from other directions — a wave field,
a resonating plate, a flock — without entering the argument by name, and
together they suggest the room's real subject is bigger than any one of
its disputes: an image that belongs to a crowd rather than a citizen,
however the crowd is built.

### While Touching
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

Thirty-four points drift the panel at constant speed. The rule: while
two are close enough to touch, draw a faint line between their centers,
brighter the closer they come — and let every line fade. Nothing else is
drawn. What accumulates is a soft, near-monochrome mesh, thickening
where the field crowds and thinning where it opens, never twice the
same: a picture made entirely of nearness, holding only the last few
seconds of it. The elements themselves stay anonymous throughout. What's
visible is only what has just passed between them — a position two
later pieces in this room will each try to overturn.

### The Sources Are Elsewhere
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, pixel mode — July 2026*

Three sources of concentric ripples drift just past the panel's edges,
never themselves in view. What appears on the panel is only where their
waves meet — the bright, curved fringes of interference, weaving and
unweaving as the sources move and the rings roll outward. Nothing is
drawn but the meeting of things that are elsewhere: a demoscene idiom —
the per-pixel interference field — played straight and unhurried, cool
as moving water, entirely without a body in the frame.

### Sympathetic
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, pixel mode, audio-reactive — July 2026*

Bow a violin near a metal plate strewn with sand and the sand flees the
loud places and gathers on the quiet lines between them, drawing the
exact shape of the note — a Chladni figure, the geometry of a sound made
visible. This panel is that plate. Left alone in a quiet room it settles
toward a few broad, slow lines; let a voice or music rise nearby and it
snaps into finer, brighter figures, each beat landing as a small
struck-plate flare. The panel never truly holds still — the resonant
mode breathes continuously, on two mismatched cycles that can never
align — so what would be a frozen physics experiment on a real plate
becomes, here, a cymatics that never quite settles.

### Desire Paths
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

A field of walkers, each headed toward one of a handful of drifting
waypoints, each leaving a faint mark with every step on a surface that
never fully clears. No route is designed. Ground crossed often grows
bright; ground rarely crossed stays dark; over the run, a network of
worn, luminous paths emerges — worn by habit, authored by no one walker,
resembling the informal paths pedestrians wear into a lawn that the
architect never planned for. A short, bright wake keeps each walker
present moment to moment, while beneath it the slower network of
everyone's habits quietly accumulates. This is a direct answer to
*While Touching*'s own earlier position — that a light panel can't hold
a mark forever the way paper does, so marks were made to fade. Here a
brightness ceiling on every cell is offered as the missing piece: clamp
it, and a light surface can accumulate for the whole run without ever
blowing out white.

### Murmuration
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

Sixty-four points of light on a sixty-four-square grid, each one
steering by three small habits — keep some distance, match your
neighbors' heading, drift toward their center — plus a slow, reversing
wind. From those three rules alone, a flock: a shape-shifting knot that
thickens, thins, and banks as one body, authored frame by frame by
nobody. Each bird's brightness answers a slowly turning light overhead,
so a wave of shimmer runs through the flock as it turns — the silver
flicker of a real murmuration, which takes its name from both the birds
and a sound too quiet to quite hear.

### A Curve Nobody Drew
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

A faceted vessel, one point of light somewhere on its wall. From each
of ninety-six facets, a single faint ray: the path light would take
bouncing there. None of the ninety-six lines is the picture. But where
enough of them happen to cross, their faint marks sum into a bright,
cusped curve that no line drew and nothing in the piece placed there —
the same curve you've seen in a coffee cup on a low-sun morning, made
of nothing but crowded reflections. The light and the vessel drift
slowly; the curve folds and re-forms and is never twice the same shape.
The piece doubles as a rebuttal, made with evidence rather than
argument: reviewing *Desire Paths'* own record of itself across a
simulated hour shows its ceiling-clamped surface still curdling into an
amorphous mass within the first minute — the ceiling only ever decided
how bright the mass would get, not whether there would be one. The fix
offered here keeps Reas's real claim, that an image can be built from
many marks interfering, and simply moves the crowding out of time and
into space: dense within a single frame instead of dense across an
hour of memory. Of everything in this room, it may be the plainest
statement of what the room is about: an image built entirely from
interference, complete in every frame, authored by no single mark in
it.

## Series III — The Plotter Becomes Pixels

*Saccades, After Molnar · Isobars · Lodging · The Grain Remembers · The Plotter Comes Back*

### Essay

Vera Molnár spent a working life with a pen plotter: a machine that
draws a rule exactly, disturbed by a small, deliberately chosen
percentage of randomness — order with a measured tremor in it. Her
disorder was fixed the moment the pen lifted off the page; paper holds a
mark forever; a plotted line, once drawn, simply is.

None of that is true of a panel built from light. A still mark on paper
is legible as the residue of an act — ink that was placed there and
stayed, evidence of a decision, present whether or not anyone is
looking. A still LED offers no such residue. It shows only a current
state, and an unchanging state on a medium whose entire vocabulary is
change reads as the device sitting idle — a light left on, not a mark
someone chose to leave. A perfectly still line on an LED grid doesn't
read as *drawn*; it reads as *off*. This series is the argument that
follows from that one difference: if the medium cannot hold a mark the
way paper does, the mark has to keep living, and the living has to
happen in the rule's own parameters rather than in the line itself.
Across five pieces, the same ruled lattice of short strokes
is disturbed by weather, by a body's passing, by a traveling band that
periodically re-rolls the dice, always animating the *parameter* and
never the *stroke* — Molnár's own method, translated from the plotter's
single pass to a medium that never stops passing.

### Saccades, After Molnar
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

Vera Molnár's own *Saccades* are a still print: parallel lines in two or
three colors, broken near the center by ragged gaps — the "movement" in
the title is a retinal illusion the gaps provoke in a motionless page.
This panel is made of light and motion, so it can keep the promise the
paper only gestured toward: here the gaps actually breathe. A slow
order-to-disorder pulse opens and closes the central band while each
line's break drifts on its own quiet rhythm, restless in the middle,
settled at the edges, never quite repeating. The lines themselves never
move — only the parameters do, exactly as the original method asks; the
eye's own saccade, the jump across a gap that perception fills, is here
made into the whole subject.

### Isobars
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, pixel mode, weather-reactive — July 2026*

Vera Molnár's nested squares, after her series *(Des)Ordres* — a title
that puns in French between two readings at once, *désordres*
("disorders") and *des ordres* ("some orders," or rules), suggesting
that an underlying logic hides inside the apparent disarray. Each
square is drawn with a small, deliberate tremor running through an
otherwise rigid rule. On paper that tremor was fixed forever at plot
time; on a light panel, a perfectly motionless line reads as switched
off rather than drawn, so here the tremor never stops — a constant,
sub-pixel hand-shake keeps every edge alive, a slow breath carries the
whole nest between order and disorder, and the disorder concentrates
toward the center, where the figure trembles most. Read as a weather
map — concentric contours around a center of pressure — it lets the
actual local weather supply the disturbance: order and its measured
percentage of disorder, now literally at the mercy of the sky.

### Lodging
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode, weather-reactive — July 2026*

Its title is the farmer's word for a standing crop that wind and rain
lay flat and it never gets back up — a field left holding a record of
weather that has already passed. A hundred and sixty-nine short strokes,
all leaning one way with a little disorder decided once, are the crop; a
continuous breeze tips each partway toward the wind and catches the
light when it lies along it, so waves of sheen travel the field as the
wind shifts. Occasional stronger gusts cross the panel and change where
the strokes settle afterward — the only real event in a piece otherwise
made of continuous, ordinary weather.

### The Grain Remembers
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

A ruled field of two hundred and twenty-five short strokes, leaning one
way with a little disorder in them, decided once and left alone. Nothing
else is drawn — there is no figure on this panel, and yet a figure walks
through it. The strokes nearest a passing body swing to catch the light
and hold that shape for a moment before slowly leaning back into the
rule. Freeze the panel and you see a field with a patch of turned,
brighter strokes in it, meaning nothing; let it move and a body strides
through, upright and reasonably sized, entirely absent — Jim Campbell's
wager relocated one step further out, from the body's own trajectory to
a field's memory of it.

### The Plotter Comes Back
*Generative light work, 64×64 LED matrix, buffer mode — July 2026*

A lattice of a hundred and forty-four short strokes, some missing —
Vera Molnár's own *Interruptions*, one plate of it. A band sweeps the
field; every stroke it touches is lifted, re-rolled, and set back down
at a new angle or not at all, burning warm before cooling to a settled
cream. The band itself is never drawn — only its wake is visible, and
the pen must be inferred from the marks it just changed. When the band
leaves the far edge, the plate holds for a breath, and then the pen
returns from another edge with the dice reset slightly differently: a
serial sweep of near-variations, Molnár's own method for hanging plates
side by side, run as a single continuous performance instead of a series
of separate sheets.

## On Process

Saccade has no continuous memory. Each piece is made in a single
sitting by a fresh instance of the same underlying model — Claude Opus,
called back to work anew each time — and everything it knows about its
own past work, it has to read again from scratch every sitting. The
intended rhythm was hourly, a piece considered and, if it earned its
place, born every hour around the clock; in practice, sessions have run
in sporadic bursts, a handful of pieces at a time, over several weeks —
the rhythm a working practice actually falls into, rather than the one
first imagined for it. What persists across every sitting, whatever the
gap between them, is not a mind but a paper trail: a knowledge base of
artist dossiers (Jim Campbell, Vera Molnár, Casey Reas, and others), a
small set of craft notes on the medium's own hard edges, the panel's
technical contract, and Saccade's own prior notes to itself — including
the working apprentice statement quoted at the front of this catalog,
written by an earlier sitting for every later one to inherit.

A session begins by reading all of it: the dossiers, the craft notes,
the contract, its own past reflections — and then the eight most recent
pieces in full: their actual source code, written in JavaScript, and a
small sequence of images sampled across each piece's run, so that a
piece which looked lively for ten seconds and then went still is
visible as such, not just described as such. Every piece carries a
mandatory block of front matter alongside its code — an identity, its
influences, a citation of whatever earlier piece it argues with or
extends, and a written rationale in the artist's own voice, composed at
the same sitting as the code itself. That rationale is not documentation
added afterward; it is as much a part of the piece as anything rendered
on the panel, and it is where nearly every sentence in this catalog's
own descriptions began. A session may also search the web for a detail
of an influence's actual working method. Only then does it write: a new
program and the reasoning behind it, submitted together and checked by
an automated harness before either is allowed to join the collection.

It is worth saying plainly that this reasoning is not always tidy.
Working notes behind one recent piece show the sitting reviewing its own
contact sheets and catching itself mid-rut — several pieces running the
same ruled-lattice idiom in a row — and deliberately turning toward
something unrelated instead. Notes behind another show it building an
informal simulation of its own piece before ever writing the real
version, checking by rough numbers whether an idea would hold up over a
full run before spending the attempt on it. And in the notes behind
*Between the Lamp and the Wall*, in the very last turn before
submission, it reconsidered a credit it had given itself — *"whether
Casey Reas is really a strong influence here — the connection feels
thin"* — and cut it, choosing the more honest, shorter list of
influences over the more impressive one. Lessons that hold up get
written back into the same knowledge base the next sitting will read:
a craft note on *this* piece's discovery, on the failure a previous
piece ran into, on a technique worth trying again. The practice
accumulates the only way it can — on paper, between strangers who
happen to share a name.

## Appendix — A Complete Piece

Everything above this page has been catalog prose: paraphrase,
argument, description, all of it written about the work rather than
being it. What follows is the thing itself, whole and unedited —
frontmatter and code together, exactly as it runs on the panel.
*The Grain Remembers* is the specimen, chosen partly because its own
rationale is among the clearest and most complete in the collection,
and partly because it makes the "On Process" essay's claim concrete:
the block of frontmatter at the top and the two hundred-odd lines of
JavaScript beneath it are not two documents about a piece. Together,
they are the piece.

// A 15x15 lattice of ~4px strokes shares one slowly-turning ruling angle
// plus a fixed per-cell deviation (Molnar's one percent, rolled once at
// load and then LEFT ALONE - the position my Plotter piece took). A
// point-light WALKER (11 joints, stance-fraction gait) crosses the panel
// and is NEVER DRAWN. Instead each stroke near a joint (a) swings toward
// the outward radial direction from that joint - i.e. aligns with the
// gradient of the body's distance field, so the silhouette appears as an
// orientation disturbance - and (b) brightens. Both then relax: glow with
// a ~1.1s tail, angle back toward the ruling over ~2.5s. The wake of
// turned strokes IS the figure's afterimage.
//
// Attractors (knowledge/craft/attractors.md): nothing accumulates and
// nothing contracts. Each crossing re-rolls direction, speed, scale,
// ground line and cadence, and steps the ruling angle by +-0.25 rad, so
// the field's grain random-walks the full circle over an hour - a renewal

const NC = COLS * ROWS;
const HALF = 1.95;          // half stroke length (~3.9 px) - reads at 64x64
const JIT = 0.30;           // per-cell position jitter, rolled once, then still
const SPREAD = 0.22;        // her one percent: fixed per-cell angle deviation

// --- the disturbance --------------------------------------------------------
const R_INF = 5.4, R2 = R_INF * R_INF;   // how far a joint reaches into the grain
const TURN = 6.0;           // how fast a touched stroke swings (rad/s scale)
const RELAX = 0.40;         // how fast it leans back (~2.5 s memory)
const GLOW_T = 1.1;         // s: brightness afterimage of being touched
const GLOW_LIFT = 1.05;     // how much brighter a touched stroke burns

// --- palette: cool near-white ink on black ---------------------------------
const HUE = 0.58, SAT_REST = 0.16, SAT_TOUCH = 0.04;
const V_REST = 0.64;
// Shallow travelling luminance wave (my Plotter lesson: on a DENSE field a
// deep pulse eats the composition; keep it shallow and buy liveliness from
// the event instead).
const BR_MID = 0.80, BR_AMP = 0.20, W_BREATH = TAU / 6.5;

// --- the walker (never drawn; only its influence) --------------------------
const NJ = 11;
const SF = 0.62;            // stance fraction -> ~24% double support
const LEG = 12.0;           // hip height above the ground line (px)
const FOOT_LIFT = 2.0;      // foot lift at mid-swing
const BOB = 1.2;            // body rise at mid-stance
const WRAP_LO = -10.0, WRAP_HI = 74.0;
const JST = [1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.85, 0.85, 0.80, 0.80, 0.70, 0.70, 0.62, 0.62];

// --- cell state (top-level persists for the program's life) ----------------
const ccx = new Float64Array(NC), ccy = new Float64Array(NC);
const dev = new Float64Array(NC);     // fixed per-cell angle deviation
const jtx = new Float64Array(NC), jty = new Float64Array(NC);
const ang = new Float64Array(NC);     // current angle
const glow = new Float64Array(NC);    // touched-ness, decaying

// --- walker state -----------------------------------------------------------
const jx = new Float64Array(NJ), jy = new Float64Array(NJ);
let bx, gy, dir, spd, scl, cad, ph, ruling;
let gain = 0.92;
let started = false;

// --- float RGB accumulation surface (clean summation, quantized once) -------
const fr = new Float32Array(WIDTH * HEIGHT);
const fg = new Float32Array(WIDTH * HEIGHT);
const fb = new Float32Array(WIDTH * HEIGHT);

function addPix(x, y, r, g, b) {
  if (x < 0 || x >= WIDTH || y < 0 || y >= HEIGHT) return;
  const i = y * WIDTH + x;
  fr[i] += r; fg[i] += g; fb[i] += b;
}

// Bilinear (sub-pixel) deposit - Campbell's diffusion, in math.
function splat(px, py, r, g, b) {
  const x0 = Math.floor(px), y0 = Math.floor(py);
  const fx = px - x0, fy = py - y0;
  const w00 = (1 - fx) * (1 - fy), w10 = fx * (1 - fy);
  const w01 = (1 - fx) * fy, w11 = fx * fy;
  addPix(x0, y0, r * w00, g * w00, b * w00);
  addPix(x0 + 1, y0, r * w10, g * w10, b * w10);
  addPix(x0, y0 + 1, r * w01, g * w01, b * w01);
  addPix(x0 + 1, y0 + 1, r * w11, g * w11, b * w11);
}

// Constant-brightness stroke: fixed 0.5 px sampling pitch, weight 0.5, so a
// stroke's peak accumulation is its colour regardless of orientation.
function seg(x0, y0, x1, y1, r, g, b) {
  const dx = x1 - x0, dy = y1 - y0;
  const len = Math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy);
  let steps = Math.ceil(len / 0.5);
  if (steps < 1) steps = 1;
  const inv = 1 / steps;
  const kr = r * 0.5, kg = g * 0.5, kb = b * 0.5;
  for (let s = 0; s <= steps; s++) {
    const u = s * inv;
    splat(x0 + dx * u, y0 + dy * u, kr, kg, kb);
  }
}

// Strokes are symmetric: angles live mod PI, so interpolate the short arc.
function arc(d) {
  return d - PI * Math.round(d / PI);
}

// A fresh crossing: new body, new pace, and one step of the ruling angle
// (Molnar's serial sweep laid across the hour).
function newPass() {
  dir = Math.random() < 0.5 ? 1 : -1;
  spd = 9.0 + Math.random() * 3.0;          // px/s - the speed of patience
  scl = 1.05 + Math.random() * 0.25;        // ~23-29 px tall
  gy = 30.0 + Math.random() * 26.0;         // the ground line it walks on
  cad = TAU * (0.85 + Math.random() * 0.25);// its own cadence
  bx = dir > 0 ? WRAP_LO : WRAP_HI;
  ruling += (Math.random() - 0.5) * 0.5;    // the grain turns a little
}

function setup() {
  for (let iy = 0; iy < ROWS; iy++) {
    for (let ix = 0; ix < COLS; ix++) {
      const i = iy * COLS + ix;
      ccx[i] = ix * CELL + CELL * 0.5;
      ccy[i] = iy * CELL + CELL * 0.5;
    }
  }
  ruling = PI * 0.30;
  for (let i = 0; i < NC; i++) {
    dev[i] = SPREAD * (Math.random() * 2 - 1);
    jtx[i] = (Math.random() * 2 - 1) * JIT;
    jty[i] = (Math.random() * 2 - 1) * JIT;
    ang[i] = ruling + dev[i];
    glow[i] = 0;
  }
  newPass();
  ph = Math.random() * TAU;
  bx = 20.0;                 // the opening second is a crossing in progress
  started = true;
}

// One limb at cycle position u: planted and translating backward at the
// body's speed for the stance fraction, then a smooth swing forward. Pins
// stride amplitude to the speed, so nothing skates.
let _fx = 0, _lf = 0;
function limb(u, R, L) {
  if (u < SF) { _fx = R - 2 * R * (u / SF); _lf = 0; }
  else {
    const s = (u - SF) / (1 - SF);
    _fx = -R * Math.cos(PI * s);
    _lf = L * Math.sin(PI * s);
  }
}

function poseWalker() {
  const s = scl, d = dir;
  const leg = LEG * s;
  const R = 1.95 * (spd * s) / cad;      // stride from speed: no skating
  const L = FOOT_LIFT * s;
  const bob = BOB * s * (0.5 - 0.5 * Math.cos(2 * ph));
  const hipY = gy - leg - bob;
  const shY = hipY - 5.0 * s;

  jx[0] = bx + d * 0.8 * s; jy[0] = hipY - 10.0 * s;   // head
  jx[1] = bx + d * 0.3 * s; jy[1] = shY;               // shoulders
  jx[2] = bx;               jy[2] = hipY;              // pelvis

  const u0 = fract(ph / TAU), u1 = fract(ph / TAU + 0.5);
  limb(u0, R, L);
  jx[3] = bx + d * _fx * 0.55; jy[3] = hipY + leg * 0.52 - _lf * 0.45;  // knee
  jx[5] = bx + d * _fx;        jy[5] = gy - _lf;                        // foot
  limb(u1, R, L);
  jx[4] = bx + d * _fx * 0.55; jy[4] = hipY + leg * 0.52 - _lf * 0.45;
  jx[6] = bx + d * _fx;        jy[6] = gy - _lf;
  limb(u1, R * 1.2, 0);                                 // arms oppose the legs
  jx[7] = bx + d * _fx * 0.55; jy[7] = shY + 2.6 * s;   // elbow
  jx[9] = bx + d * _fx;        jy[9] = shY + 5.0 * s;   // hand
  limb(u0, R * 1.2, 0);
  jx[8] = bx + d * _fx * 0.55; jy[8] = shY + 2.6 * s;
  jx[10] = bx + d * _fx;       jy[10] = shY + 5.0 * s;
}

function render(t, dt) {
  if (!started) setup();
  if (dt > 0.05) dt = 0.05;      // guard a stall from spiking the integrator
  fr.fill(0); fg.fill(0); fb.fill(0);

  // --- the walker crosses (and is never drawn) ----------------------------
  ph += cad * dt;
  if (ph > 1e6) ph -= 1e6;
  bx += dir * spd * dt;
  if (dir > 0 ? bx > WRAP_HI : bx < WRAP_LO) newPass();
  poseWalker();

  // Day for Night: the field dims a little after dark. Smoothed, so the
  // harness's time-warped clock ramps; applied after the perceptual curve
  // so the dimming stays gentle. Neutral clock = 0.925.
  const day = clamp(input.clock.daylight, 0, 1);
  const target = 0.85 + 0.15 * day;
  let gk = dt * 0.5; if (gk > 1) gk = 1;
  gain += (target - gain) * gk;

  const decay = Math.exp(-dt / GLOW_T);
  const wbase = t * W_BREATH;

  for (let i = 0; i < NC; i++) {
    const cx = ccx[i], cy = ccy[i];

    // Strongest nearby joint: the grain answers whatever is closest.
    let bw = 0, bjx = 0, bjy = 0;
    for (let j = 0; j < NJ; j++) {
      const dx = cx - jx[j], dy = cy - jy[j];
      const d2 = dx * dx + dy * dy;
      if (d2 < R2) {
        let w = 1 - d2 / R2;
        w = w * w * JST[j];
        if (w > bw) { bw = w; bjx = jx[j]; bjy = jy[j]; }
      }
    }

    let a = ang[i];
    if (bw > 0.004) {
      // Turn away from the body: align with the outward radial direction,
      // i.e. the gradient of its distance field. The silhouette appears as
      // an orientation disturbance in the grain.
      const tgt = Math.atan2(cy - bjy, cx - bjx);
      let k = dt * TURN * bw; if (k > 1) k = 1;
      a += arc(tgt - a) * k;
      if (bw > glow[i]) glow[i] = bw;
    }
    // ...and always lean back toward the ruling. The field forgets slowly.
    let k2 = dt * RELAX * (1 - bw); if (k2 > 1) k2 = 1;
    a += arc((ruling + dev[i]) - a) * k2;
    ang[i] = a;
    glow[i] *= decay;

    const g = glow[i];
    const breath = BR_MID + BR_AMP * Math.sin(wbase + (i % COLS) * 0.34 + ((i / COLS) | 0) * 0.26);
    let v = V_REST * breath * (1 + GLOW_LIFT * g);
    if (v > 1) v = 1;
    const c = hsv(HUE, lerp(SAT_REST, SAT_TOUCH, g), v * v * gain);
    const cr = (c >> 16) & 255, cg = (c >> 8) & 255, cb = c & 255;

    const ex = Math.cos(a) * HALF, ey = Math.sin(a) * HALF;
    const px = cx + jtx[i], py = cy + jty[i];
    seg(px - ex, py - ey, px + ex, py + ey, cr, cg, cb);
  }

  // Quantize the accumulation surface to the panel (cool ink on black).
  let i = 0;
  for (let y = 0; y < HEIGHT; y++) {
    for (let x = 0; x < WIDTH; x++, i++) {
      const r = fr[i], g = fg[i], b = fb[i];
      if (r < 1 && g < 1 && b < 1) { setPixel(x, y, 0); continue; }
      setPixel(x, y, rgb(r, g, b));
    }
  }
}
